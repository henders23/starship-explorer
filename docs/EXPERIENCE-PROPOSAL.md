# Experience and narrative proposals

The map mock-up deliberately borrows only the reference's **visual grammar**—a luminous
lane network, deep-space backdrop, framed instrument panels, and immediately legible
points of interest. Its factions, war state, missions, system classifications, and lore
were not copied. The chart remains a view of Starship Ithaca's generated galaxy and
its evidence-driven journey home.

## First-session experience

- **Open on a playable crisis.** Let the player make one urgent bridge decision before
  the title card, then reveal that the anomaly has stranded the ship. This teaches the
  game's tone through action rather than exposition.
- **Teach one question at a time.** The first three destinations should introduce travel,
  evidence, and trust separately. Keep the full Evidence Board visually quiet until the
  first account is collected.
- **Give the opening a human anchor.** Establish three officers with conflicting opinions
  during the briefing; reuse their voices in tooltips and decisions so the UI feels like
  a crew, not a manual.
- **Offer a short and a full briefing.** Returning players should be able to take command
  immediately without losing the fiction of entering the bridge.

## Map and navigation

- **Make intent visible before commitment.** Hovering a system should preview route,
  fuel, elapsed days, known risk, and which crew will recover while travelling.
- **Turn layers into player tools.** Add toggles for evidence, resources, hazards, and
  crew rumours; remember those choices between visits to the chart.
- **Let players annotate the sky.** Pins such as “likely lie,” “fuel stop,” and “return
  later” support deduction without forcing notes outside the game.
- **Reveal the map through certainty, not darkness.** Keep public astronomy visible while
  contacts, threats, and opportunities resolve from uncertain signals into known facts.
- **Celebrate route changes.** When trusting or rejecting an account changes the candidate
  set, animate the affected stars and let the responsible clue briefly speak in the log.
- **Support keyboard and reduced-motion play.** Every system should be focusable and
  selectable, with searchable names, zoom shortcuts, strong focus states, and a static
  alternative to map motion.

## Narrative and pacing

- **Give each region a dramatic question.** For example: whom can we trust, what will we
  sacrifice for fuel, and what did the anomaly do to us? Encounters can vary while still
  advancing a recognizable chapter.
- **Build recurring relationships.** Survivors, rivals, and traders should remember prior
  choices and return in later regions. A second meeting makes an earlier decision feel
  authored even in a generated galaxy.
- **Let crew losses alter scenes.** Replace missing specialists with awkward workarounds,
  grief conversations, and changes in who argues on the bridge—not only reduced stats.
- **Use the captain's log as a story recap.** Group entries into discoveries, promises,
  losses, and open questions, with links back to the relevant system and evidence.
- **Put quiet beats after costly events.** A short bridge exchange after a death, failed
  jump, or major discovery gives consequences room to land before the next decision.
- **Foreshadow every ending.** Recurring sensory details and crew theories should make the
  final truth surprising but retrospectively coherent.

## Motivation and replay

- **State a near-term objective alongside “find home.”** A changing bridge priority—secure
  fuel, decode the beacon, reach the wounded contact—keeps the middle of a run purposeful.
- **Reward understanding rather than collection.** Unlock deductions, dialogue options,
  and safer plans when evidence is combined correctly; avoid turning lore into a checklist.
- **Make failure specific.** An epilogue should cite the trusted lie, ignored warning,
  decisive loss, and surviving relationships so defeat closes this captain's story.
- **Seed replay hooks in fiction.** After an ending, surface one unanswered signal or
  alternate testimony from the same seed rather than offering only a generic restart.
